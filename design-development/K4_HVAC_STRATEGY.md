# K4 HVAC / Ventilation Strategy

Status: **MEP design intent only — equipment and capacities require room-by-room heat-load calculation**

## Recommendation

Do **not** force a whole-apartment fully ducted central system under the 2.50 m clear-ceiling constraint.

Preferred direction is a **hybrid zoned system**:

- open-plan living/dining/kitchen: concealed ducted / slim-duct study using the local kitchen-tall-wall service pocket;
- master suite: investigate a localized concealed unit in a service-friendly dressing/hall zone, not a blanket dropped master-bedroom ceiling;
- Bedroom 1: high-wall split unless a measured concealed route proves clearly better;
- Bedroom 2: high-wall split unless a measured concealed route proves clearly better;
- bathrooms/WC: ventilation/extract as required; no comfort supply unless the final MEP engineer deliberately includes it;
- kitchen hood/exhaust remains a completely separate system from comfort AC.

This preserves height, simplifies maintenance and gives independent zoning rather than lowering every room for a visual idea.

## Open-plan zone

K4 retains only one localized ceiling/service pocket aligned with the kitchen tall units.

Current reservation concept:
- pocket aligned to tall-unit face rather than projecting into the room;
- underside approximately 2.34 m in the exploratory model;
- verified concrete underside reference is 2.63 m, giving roughly 290 mm gross vertical reservation before actual build-ups.

This is enough to justify **studying** slim concealed equipment, not enough to certify a specific unit.

Required engineering:
1. actual cooling/heating load for the ~47.5 m² reception plus open kitchen contribution;
2. equipment capacity / sound data;
3. supply/return air quantities;
4. grille/slot locations and throw;
5. duct size / pressure loss;
6. filter and service access;
7. condensate gravity route or pump;
8. outdoor-unit location;
9. kitchen hood interaction / pressure balance;
10. acoustic lining / vibration control.

Do not size by room area alone.

## Master suite

Preferred sequence:
1. test a compact concealed unit in/above the dressing or a localized circulation/service zone;
2. supply master bedroom with short duct / discreet grille arrangement;
3. retain the master bedroom main ceiling at 2.50 m wherever possible;
4. keep service access outside the sleeping focal ceiling if feasible.

Fallback: high-wall split on a solid wall with concealed pipe/drain route. A good wall split is preferable to a badly proportioned false ceiling.

## Bedrooms 1 and 2

Default recommendation: efficient high-wall inverter splits.

Reasons:
- independent control;
- low ceiling preserved;
- simpler maintenance;
- no need to create deep ceiling zones in small rooms;
- lower construction complexity.

Coordinate indoor units so they:
- do not blow directly at the pillow/desk for long periods;
- have a reliable condensate fall;
- remain serviceable;
- do not conflict with full-height curtains or wardrobes.

## Outdoor units

Locations are **TBD** until developer/façade rules and actual designated condenser/service areas are confirmed.

Do not assume the service terrace can accept every outdoor unit.

## Fresh air / ventilation

Comfort AC recirculation is not fresh air.

- bathrooms/WC: extract strategy to be confirmed;
- kitchen: ducted hood to exterior / approved shaft route, independent from AC;
- open-plan make-up air / infiltration implications reviewed with the hood capacity;
- operable glazing and normal natural ventilation remain useful but do not replace required mechanical ventilation where applicable.

## Controls

- independent thermostat/control by major zone;
- living zone should support quiet/night and normal modes;
- bedrooms individually controllable;
- avoid one thermostat governing the whole apartment.

## Ceiling rule

No ceiling drop is justified solely to “make AC look concealed.”

A dropped zone must have a real equipment/duct/access purpose and must be shown on coordinated MEP + reflected-ceiling drawings before gypsum construction.
